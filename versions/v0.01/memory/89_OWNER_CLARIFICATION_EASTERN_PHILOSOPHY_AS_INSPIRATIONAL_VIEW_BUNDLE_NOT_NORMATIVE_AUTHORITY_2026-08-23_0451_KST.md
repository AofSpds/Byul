## Owner clarification

The Owner clarified that earlier references to Buddhist / Eastern philosophical ideas were primarily used as an intuitive explanatory aid and a source of inspiration for the current relation/View hypothesis.

The intended research posture is:

- philosophical and religious systems can themselves be understood as Views / View bundles that abstract the world in particular ways;
- those abstraction methods may be useful prior conceptual references that can be borrowed, decomposed, compared, or recombined;
- the current BYUL hypothesis is not claimed to be identical to any one historical doctrine;
- no religious or philosophical tradition is to be treated as normative truth, authority, or mandatory ontology for BYUL;
- named traditions may be introduced as recognizable reference View bundles when they help explain a concept, but the project should not become fixed around them;
- the Owner believes that many of the current intuitions can be expressed using View patterns found across Buddhist and broader Eastern philosophical thought, but this is an interpretive mapping rather than an authority claim.

## Technical interpretation

A useful research framing is therefore:

```text
historical philosophy / religious worldview
    -> one or more abstraction methods / View patterns
    -> candidate inspiration / comparison surface
    -> extract useful relation / identity / change / composition intuitions
    -> test independently in BYUL
```

This is compatible with PRIOR-ART-FIRST while keeping the implementation/model-discovery program open to modern formal systems, database models, graph/hypergraph models, temporal/event systems, category/compositional approaches, cognitive science, and other sources.

## Guard

Do not infer:

`BUDDHIST OR EASTERN PHILOSOPHY -> BYUL TRUTH SOURCE`.

Do not infer:

`SIMILARITY OF EXPRESSIVE PATTERNS -> CURRENT HYPOTHESIS IS THE SAME DOCTRINE`.

Do not infer:

`REFERENCE VIEW BUNDLE -> RELIGIOUS COMMITMENT OR USER IDENTITY`.

Do not let named philosophical traditions constrain future View discovery, formalization, or implementation choices.

## Research consequence

Philosophical traditions may be catalogued as **reference View bundles** alongside technical/formal prior art, with their abstraction patterns translated into neutral research questions and then tested independently.

Examples of neutral research axes that may be inspired by such references include change/persistence, relation-dependence, non-essentialist identity, bundle/process views of self/object, contextual sameness, and layered abstraction. These are research axes only and are not validated by the historical source merely because a conceptual parallel exists.
