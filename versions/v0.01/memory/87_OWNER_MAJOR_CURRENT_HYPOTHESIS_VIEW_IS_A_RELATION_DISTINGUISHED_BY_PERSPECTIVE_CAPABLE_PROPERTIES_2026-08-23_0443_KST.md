## Owner statement

> "관계가 어떤 가진 속성에 의해 우리가 VIEW라고 구분하는 관계가 있다. 그리고 VIEW의 관계속성은 세상을 보기 위한 관점이 될수 있는 속성을 갖고 잇다. 이렇게 일단 추정하고 있습니다."

## High-fidelity interpretation

The Owner refines the current relation-centric hypothesis further.

The intended direction is not that `VIEW` is a separate ontological class placed outside the relation model.

Instead:

- all modeled constructs remain interpretable as relations / relation bundles;
- among relations, some have a configuration of relation-properties that makes it useful to distinguish them operationally as `VIEW`;
- what makes such a relation a View is that its relation-properties can function as a perspective for seeing / selecting / composing / abstracting / routing other relations and relation bundles;
- therefore `VIEW` is currently better understood as a **role or classification of certain relations**, based on their properties/capabilities, rather than as a fundamentally separate primitive type.

Conceptually:

```text
Relation r
  + relation-property bundle P
  + P has perspective-capable characteristics
  -> r may be classified / used as VIEW
```

This remains a current hypothesis, not a final ontology.

## Relation-properties and object-like attributes

The Owner has previously stated that relations and relation bundles may have object-like properties.

Under the current hypothesis, `properties` need not imply a return to object-first ontology. They may themselves be implemented or interpreted as relations / relation bundles if useful.

Therefore a View relation may have properties corresponding to research axes such as:

- scope / boundary;
- resolution;
- routing / selection / composition behavior;
- temporal treatment;
- distinction / equivalence / invariance assumptions;
- purpose / use-context;
- lifecycle interpretation;
- possibly operator-like or transformation behavior.

These are candidate research dimensions only; they are not yet an approved canonical View schema.

## Important distinction

Do not collapse the Owner statement into:

`VIEW = ATTRIBUTE OF A RELATION`.

The more accurate current reading is:

`VIEW = A RELATION THAT WE DISTINGUISH AS VIEW BECAUSE OF THE PROPERTIES/CAPABILITIES OF THAT RELATION`.

Likewise, do not infer:

`VIEW = ONE FIXED TYPE`.

The classification itself may later be View-conditioned, versioned, revised, or represented relationally.

## New research consequence

The design problem can now be phrased more precisely:

> What relation-properties or relation-capabilities are sufficient for a relation / relation bundle to act as a useful perspective over other relations, such that we operationally classify it as a VIEW?

This suggests investigating prior art for operator-like relations, higher-order relations, graph/query views, lenses, profunctor/category-inspired mappings, rule systems, relational algebra, temporal abstractions, and self-describing/meta-circular data models before fixing a bespoke View ontology.

No canonical relation property model, View classifier, operator grammar, or execution semantics is fixed by this note.
