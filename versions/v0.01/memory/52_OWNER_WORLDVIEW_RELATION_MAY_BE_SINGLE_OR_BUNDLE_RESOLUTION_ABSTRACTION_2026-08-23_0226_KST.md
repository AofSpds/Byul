## High-fidelity interpretation

- It is natural in the current worldview hypothesis for one observed relation/event to participate in multiple higher-level relation bundles/views.
- However, the Owner explicitly does **not** know whether that relation should be represented as one atomic relation or itself as another bundle of finer relations.
- This uncertainty is a core reason the Owner emphasizes **abstraction** and **resolution**.
- Therefore, apparent atomicity is not assumed to be ontologically or representationally fixed.
- A relation/event that is atomic enough at one active resolution may be decomposable into a finer relation bundle at another resolution.

Conceptually:

```text
At resolution R0:
E2 = one relation/event

At finer resolution R1:
E2 = bundle {e2.1, e2.2, e2.3, ...}

At coarser resolution R-1:
E2 may itself participate as one element of a larger bundle.
```

## Important guard

Do not infer:

`ONE OBSERVED RELATION = ALWAYS ATOMIC`

or

`EVERY RELATION MUST ALWAYS BE DECOMPOSED`

or

`RELATION BUNDLE = FIXED IMPLEMENTATION STRUCTURE`.

The appropriate representation/granularity remains open and should be investigated through prior art, interview reasoning, and empirical model-discovery probes.

## Relation to abstraction methodology

This sharpens the Owner's earlier formulation that abstraction asks how to view a finer relation bundle in order to meet the required performance. The research problem is therefore not only *which view* to form, but also *at what resolution a relation should be treated as atomic versus bundled* for the active requirement.

## Interview implication

A useful next unresolved question is whether different regions/relations in the same world model may coexist at different active resolutions simultaneously, rather than enforcing one global resolution.

No canonical resolution scale, bundle algebra, atomicity rule, routing strategy, chain/twin representation, or implementation requirement is fixed by this note.
