## High-fidelity interpretation

The Owner confirms two important points.

### 1. ASA's initial Persona is intentionally seeded with the Owner's strong foundational worldview

The Owner holds the current foundational worldview with a strength that other systems might encode as absolute principles or laws. However, precisely because of that worldview, those convictions are represented through relation/View semantics rather than silently hardened into absolute ontology.

The Owner is intentionally putting this Persona/worldview into the first agent, ASA, as part of its Seed Persona / Seed View configuration.

This means ASA INIT is not value-neutral or structure-neutral. It begins from a deliberate Owner-designed orientation, while the worldview itself remains capable of expressing its own beliefs as View-conditioned and revisable rather than metaphysically absolute.

### 2. Perception/world-modeling (A) and behavior/expression (B) need not be frozen as separate View objects

The immediately preceding A/B distinction was:

```text
A = how the world/source is seen, selected, preserved, discarded, composed
B = how the resulting interpretation becomes behavior, expression, judgment, interaction
```

The Owner clarifies:

- A and B may be represented as two Views for convenience;
- they may also be modeled as one larger relation-bundle/View;
- A is naturally source-like relative to B, and B target-like relative to A in one scoped relation;
- the system should not promote this convenient decomposition into a foundational law;
- chaining may produce A -> B -> A' -> B' ... or similar iterative loops, which is considered ordinary compositional behavior rather than a special ontology.

Conceptually:

```text
[A View] -> [B View]
```

or:

```text
[AB combined relation-bundle]
```

or through chaining:

```text
A_t0 -> B_t1 -> A_t2 -> B_t3 -> ...
```

The exact decomposition is implementation/View-conditioned.
