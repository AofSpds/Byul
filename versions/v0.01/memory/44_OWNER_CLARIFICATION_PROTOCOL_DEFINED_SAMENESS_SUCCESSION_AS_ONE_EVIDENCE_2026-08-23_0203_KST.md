## High-fidelity interpretation

- Succession/lineage is **one possible kind of evidence** used in deciding whether two things count as the same; it is not the universal definition of identity.
- The criterion for `same` is selected or defined by a **protocol** (broadly understood as the active comparison/interpretation rule), and different protocols may classify the same pair differently.
- Thus sameness is protocol-/criterion-relative rather than an absolute primitive relation fixed globally.
- Examples intentionally demonstrate that physical distinctness, temporal continuity, and ordinary-language identity need not line up under every protocol:
  - two distinct chopstick pieces may count as `same` under some criterion;
  - the Owner at `t-1ms` and `t` may count as different under a high-resolution temporal criterion;
  - the Owner and ASA may count as the `same person` under some protocol/view even though they are not the same physical entity.
- Do **not** strengthen the last example into a factual claim of literal human identity; it is an example of protocol-relative sameness.

## Relation to prior Owner Primary Record

The Owner Primary Record already contains the high-resolution temporal intuition:

and the correction that apparent bidirectionality/identity can be a coarse view over temporally distinct mappings.

The current turn generalizes that earlier intuition into an explicit protocol-relative identity rule and therefore should be treated as a refinement, not a new absolute ontology.

The protocol may use succession, similarity, role, function, category, purpose, context, scale, or other criteria; which are legitimate remains an empirical/design question.

## Interview-method implication

Identity/sameness has now been discussed repeatedly. Avoid further near-duplicate questions about whether lineage alone defines identity. Future interview should move to genuinely unresolved dimensions, such as the scope and lifecycle of protocols, how competing protocols coexist, or how protocol choice itself is evidenced/selected.

No canonical identity function, equivalence relation, protocol registry, or selection rule is fixed by this note.
