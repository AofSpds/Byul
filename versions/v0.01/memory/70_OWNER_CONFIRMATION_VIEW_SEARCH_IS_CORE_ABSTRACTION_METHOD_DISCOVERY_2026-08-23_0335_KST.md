## Referent being confirmed

The immediately preceding discussion clarified that the same lower-level events/relations may be bundled differently depending on how they are viewed, and that an intermediate result may appear as a purpose in one view and as a means inside a larger composition in another.

## High-fidelity interpretation

The Owner confirms that this is the central reason BYUL is searching for `View` / abstraction methods.

The research target is not merely:

- choose a fixed model;
- compress data;
- assign permanent purpose/means labels;
- find one universal abstraction.

Rather, it is to discover how relation/event bundles should be grouped, composed, and viewed so that the resulting abstraction is useful for the active purpose and requirement-performance bundle.

Conceptually:

```text
lower-level relation/event bundle
        + candidate View / abstraction method
        -> higher-level relation / representation
        -> may itself enter another composition
```

Different candidate Views may bundle the same underlying relations differently.

Thus `Purpose`, `Means`, `Object`, `Same`, `Cycle`, `Split/Merge`, and similar higher-level descriptions should not be assumed to be fixed ontological types merely because they appear in one abstraction.

## Strong research-direction clarification

`VIEW_DISCOVERY` is not a peripheral presentation/query problem. It is a central Model-Discovery problem.

A better current description of the testbed target is:

> empirically and conceptually search for abstraction/View methods that organize relation bundles at useful resolutions for the active purpose/requirements, while preserving history/provenance of what was grouped, transformed, or lost.

This also explains why the Owner has emphasized:

- relation bundles;
- abstraction;
- resolution;
- recursive composition;
- View-conditioned sameness and lifecycle labels;
- mutable Model/View/Requirement/Evaluation/Purpose;
- prior-art-first model discovery.

## Testbed implication

Open-source systems such as Grist, Joplin, TriliumNext, and other candidates should not be treated primarily as contenders for a single winner architecture.

They are better treated as empirical probes that may expose useful or harmful mechanisms for:

- grouping/bundling relations;
- preserving lower-level basis/provenance;
- recursive abstraction;
- alternative views over related evidence;
- route/view mutation after failure;
- reconstruction and semantic loss;
- operational cost of maintaining abstractions.

The testbed should therefore learn properties of `View construction` rather than merely benchmark application-level features.

## Immediate next research axis

Now that View discovery is explicitly central, the next useful question is not whether View matters, but what kind of evidence would tell us that one candidate abstraction/View is more useful than another for a given purpose — e.g. task success, reconstruction, semantic loss, compute cost, adaptability, or other requirement bundles — without assuming one global score.
